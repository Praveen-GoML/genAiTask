# FitMind AI — Frequently Asked Questions

## Can I build muscle and lose fat at the same time?

Yes, but primarily under specific conditions:
1. **Beginners** ("newbie gains") — untrained individuals can gain muscle while in a calorie deficit for the first 6–12 months.
2. **Returning athletes** after a significant break (muscle memory effect).
3. **Individuals with high body fat** (>25% for males, >30% for females) — the body can mobilise fat stores to fuel muscle protein synthesis.

For most intermediate and advanced trainees, body recomposition is very slow. A dedicated bulk-cut cycle is more time-efficient for those goals.

## How many days per week should I train?

For most people, 3–5 days of structured resistance training per week hits the sweet spot. More is not always better — recovery capacity is the limiting factor.
- Beginners: 3 days full-body
- Intermediates: 4 days (upper/lower) or 5 days (PPL)
- Advanced: 5–6 days with well-structured deload weeks

Rest days do not mean sedentary days. Light walking, mobility work, and active recovery are beneficial.

---

## Is cardio going to kill my muscle gains?

Excessive cardio on top of high-volume weight training can cause "interference effect" — reduced strength and hypertrophy gains. However, moderate cardio (3x30 min Zone 2 per week) has minimal impact on muscle gain and significant benefits for cardiovascular health, recovery, and longevity.
Key rule: Keep cardio and strength sessions separated by at least 6 hours where possible, or do cardio after lifting (not before).

---

## What is the best diet for fat loss?

The best diet is the one you can adhere to consistently. That said, evidence-based principles apply universally:
- Calorie deficit of 300–500 kcal/day
- High protein (1.8–2.2 g/kg) to preserve muscle during the cut
- Adequate fibre (25–38 g/day) for satiety and gut health
- Prioritise whole, minimally processed foods 80% of the time

Low-carb, Mediterranean, and intermittent fasting all work — they all achieve a caloric deficit through different mechanisms.

---

## How long does it take to see results?

- **Fat loss**: Visible changes in body composition typically appear at 4–8 weeks with consistent training and nutrition.
- **Strength**: Beginners see strength gains within 2–3 weeks, primarily from neural adaptations (not yet muscle growth).
- **Muscle size (hypertrophy)**: Visible muscle gains typically emerge at 8–12 weeks. Significant changes take 6–12 months.

Photos are a better progress tracker than the scale for the first 8 weeks.

---

## Do I need to train to failure?

Training to muscular failure is not required for hypertrophy, and training to failure on every set increases injury risk and slows recovery.
**Recommended approach:** Leave 2–3 reps in reserve (RIR) on most sets. Use failure or near-failure (0–1 RIR) only on the last set of an exercise as a technique to maximise stimulus.

---

## Is breakfast important for muscle gain?

The research on "breakfast" specifically is weak. What matters is total daily protein and calorie intake. However, spreading protein across 4–5 meals (including a morning meal) is better for MPS than eating the same total in 1–2 sittings.

## What is the difference between DOMS and an injury?

**DOMS (Delayed Onset Muscle Soreness):**
- Appears 24–72 hours after training, especially after novel exercises or high eccentric load.
- Feels like a diffuse, achy stiffness in the muscle belly.
- Improves with light movement, heat, and time.
- Normal and not harmful — does not indicate a more effective workout.

**Injury warning signs:**
- Sharp, stabbing, or shooting pain during exercise.
- Pain in a joint (not a muscle belly).
- Swelling, bruising, or loss of range of motion.
- Pain that worsens with movement rather than improving.
- Any of the above requires medical assessment before continuing to train.
