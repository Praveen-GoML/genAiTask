FITMIND AI — GOAL SETTING & PROGRESS TRACKING
===============================================

THE SMART FRAMEWORK FOR FITNESS GOALS
---------------------------------------
Specific: "I want to lose weight" is not specific. "I want to reduce body fat from 24% to 18%" is.
Measurable: Track progress with concrete metrics — body weight (weekly average), body fat %, 1-rep max, VO2 max, resting heart rate, photos.
Achievable: Natural muscle gain rate for males: 0.5–1 kg per month for intermediates. Fat loss: 0.5–1% body weight per week is sustainable. Set targets that are ambitious but within physiological limits.
Relevant: Align fitness goals with life priorities. A new parent may need home workout options; a competitive athlete needs sport-specific programming.
Time-bound: Set a date. "I want to run a 5K in under 30 minutes by December 1st."

BODY COMPOSITION vs. SCALE WEIGHT
------------------------------------
Scale weight fluctuates 1–3 kg per day based on water, food volume, and glycogen. Do not judge progress by a single weigh-in.
Use a 7-day rolling average for weight tracking.
Progress photos taken monthly in the same lighting and pose are often more motivating than numbers alone.
Body fat percentage methods (least to most accurate): scale impedance → skinfold calipers → DEXA scan.

KEY FITNESS BENCHMARKS BY GOAL
--------------------------------
Beginner strength standards (1RM as a multiple of body weight — male / female):
  Squat:        1.0 x BW / 0.75 x BW
  Deadlift:     1.25 x BW / 1.0 x BW
  Bench Press:  0.75 x BW / 0.5 x BW
  Overhead Press: 0.5 x BW / 0.35 x BW

Intermediate strength standards:
  Squat:        1.5 x BW / 1.1 x BW
  Deadlift:     2.0 x BW / 1.5 x BW
  Bench Press:  1.25 x BW / 0.85 x BW
  Overhead Press: 0.75 x BW / 0.55 x BW

Cardiovascular benchmarks:
  5K run — good for age 30–39: under 25 min (male), under 30 min (female)
  Resting heart rate — fit adult: 50–65 bpm; elite athlete: 40–50 bpm
  VO2 max — good for age 30–39: 42–50 ml/kg/min (male), 35–43 ml/kg/min (female)

BMI REFERENCE (not ideal alone but widely used)
-------------------------------------------------
Underweight: < 18.5
Normal weight: 18.5–24.9
Overweight: 25.0–29.9
Obese Class I: 30.0–34.9
Obese Class II: 35.0–39.9
Obese Class III: ≥ 40.0
Note: BMI does not account for muscle mass. A muscular athlete may have a "overweight" BMI while having low body fat.

PERIODISATION PRINCIPLES
--------------------------
Linear Periodisation: Gradually increase weight while keeping rep range the same. Best for beginners (predictable, simple).
Undulating Periodisation (DUP): Vary rep ranges across days or weeks (e.g., Mon: 5 reps, Wed: 10 reps, Fri: 15 reps). Better for intermediates and advanced athletes to prevent adaptation.
Block Periodisation: 3–6 week blocks each focused on a different quality (hypertrophy block → strength block → power block). Used by athletes with competition schedules.

OVERCOMING PLATEAUS
---------------------
A true strength plateau is 3+ weeks with no progress on the same exercise.
Strategies: Change rep range, change exercise variation (e.g., swap barbell squat for pause squat), add volume, add frequency, implement a deload, audit sleep and nutrition — these are usually the culprit.

HABIT STACKING FOR CONSISTENCY
--------------------------------
Attach new fitness habits to existing ones: "After I brew my morning coffee, I will lay out my gym clothes." Reduce friction. Never miss twice — one missed session is an accident; two in a row is the start of a pattern.
