FITMIND AI — SUPPLEMENT GUIDE
==============================

TIER 1: WELL-EVIDENCED, SAFE, WIDELY USED
-------------------------------------------
Creatine Monohydrate
  Dose: 3–5 g per day, any time. No loading phase required.
  Evidence: One of the most studied sports supplements. Increases phosphocreatine stores in muscle, improving high-intensity power output, strength gains, and recovery between sets.
  Notes: Causes water retention in muscle (not subcutaneous fat) — expect +1–2 kg on the scale initially. Monohydrate is as effective as more expensive forms (HCL, ethyl ester).

Whey Protein
  Dose: 20–40 g per serving; use to fill protein gaps in diet. Not a replacement for whole food.
  Evidence: High bioavailability and leucine content make it ideal post-workout. Whey isolate is lower lactose and fat than concentrate — preferred for those with lactose sensitivity.
  Notes: Casein protein is slower-digesting; useful before bed to sustain overnight MPS.

Caffeine
  Dose: 3–6 mg/kg body weight 45–60 minutes pre-workout.
  Evidence: Proven ergogenic aid. Reduces perceived exertion, improves endurance, power output, and reaction time.
  Notes: Tolerance builds quickly; consider cycling (5 days on / 2 days off). Avoid within 6 hours of sleep. Max recommended daily dose: 400 mg for healthy adults.

TIER 2: USEFUL FOR SPECIFIC GOALS / POPULATIONS
-------------------------------------------------
Beta-Alanine
  Dose: 3.2–6.4 g per day. Split dose to reduce tingling (paraesthesia) side effect.
  Evidence: Increases carnosine levels in muscle, buffering acid accumulation during high-intensity efforts lasting 1–4 minutes. Most useful for 400 m–800 m runners, CrossFit, and similar.

Citrulline Malate
  Dose: 6–8 g pure L-citrulline (or 8–10 g citrulline malate) 60 min pre-workout.
  Evidence: Increases arginine bioavailability and nitric oxide production, improving blood flow, pump, and endurance. More effective than arginine directly.

Fish Oil (Omega-3 EPA+DHA)
  Dose: 2–3 g combined EPA+DHA per day.
  Evidence: Anti-inflammatory, reduces DOMS, supports heart health, joint health, and may modestly improve body composition over time.

Vitamin D3
  Dose: 1,000–4,000 IU per day depending on sun exposure and baseline blood levels. Take with fat for absorption.
  Evidence: Deficiency is extremely common (~40% of people globally). Low vitamin D is linked to reduced strength, impaired immune function, and poor bone density. Optimal blood level: 40–60 ng/mL (100–150 nmol/L).

Magnesium Glycinate / Malate
  Dose: 200–400 mg before bed.
  Evidence: Supports sleep quality, muscle relaxation, and over 300 enzymatic reactions. Athletes who sweat heavily are at higher risk of deficiency.

TIER 3: LIMITED OR CONDITIONAL EVIDENCE
-----------------------------------------
BCAAs (Branched-Chain Amino Acids)
  Generally unnecessary if protein intake is adequate (>1.6 g/kg/day). May be useful during fasted training to reduce muscle protein breakdown.

HMB (Beta-Hydroxy Beta-Methylbutyrate)
  Shows modest anti-catabolic effects in untrained individuals or during extreme caloric deficits. Effect size is small compared to creatine.

Pre-Workout Blends
  Often contain underdosed active ingredients padded with proprietary blends. Better to build your own stack: caffeine + citrulline + beta-alanine if those fit your goals.

NOT RECOMMENDED
----------------
Testosterone Boosters: Most have little to no evidence for meaningful hormone changes in healthy adults.
Fat Burners: Most are just stimulant blends. The fat-burning effect beyond caffeine is negligible.
Detox Products: The liver and kidneys handle detoxification. No supplement meaningfully accelerates this process.
