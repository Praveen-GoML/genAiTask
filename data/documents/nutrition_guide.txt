FITMIND AI — NUTRITION GUIDE
==============================

MACRONUTRIENTS
--------------
Protein: The primary macronutrient for muscle repair and growth. Aim for 1.6–2.2 g per kg of body weight per day for active individuals. Sources: chicken breast, eggs, Greek yogurt, lentils, tofu, whey protein, tuna.

Carbohydrates: The body's preferred energy source, especially for high-intensity exercise. Complex carbs (oats, brown rice, sweet potato, quinoa) provide sustained energy. Simple carbs (fruit, white rice) are useful immediately post-workout to replenish glycogen quickly.

Fats: Essential for hormone production, joint lubrication, and fat-soluble vitamin absorption (A, D, E, K). Prioritise unsaturated fats: avocado, olive oil, nuts, fatty fish. Limit saturated fats and avoid trans fats.

MICRONUTRIENTS FOR ATHLETES
-----------------------------
Iron: Critical for oxygen transport. Deficiency leads to fatigue and reduced endurance. Found in red meat, spinach, lentils, fortified cereals. Pair plant iron with vitamin C to improve absorption.

Calcium & Vitamin D: Work together for bone density. Athletes under stress fracture risk must ensure adequate intake. Sources: dairy, fortified plant milks, salmon, sunlight exposure.

Magnesium: Supports muscle contraction, nerve function, and sleep quality. Often depleted by sweat. Found in dark leafy greens, nuts, seeds, dark chocolate.

Zinc: Supports immune function and testosterone production. Found in beef, pumpkin seeds, chickpeas.

HYDRATION
----------
Dehydration of just 2% of body weight can reduce athletic performance by up to 20%. General guideline: 35–45 ml per kg of body weight per day. Increase intake on training days and in hot environments. Electrolytes (sodium, potassium, magnesium) become important during sessions exceeding 60 minutes.

PRE-WORKOUT NUTRITION
----------------------
Eat a balanced meal 2–3 hours before training: moderate carbs, moderate protein, low fat and fibre (to avoid GI discomfort). Example: grilled chicken with rice and vegetables.
If eating within 60 minutes of training: small, easily digested snack — banana with peanut butter, or a protein bar.

POST-WORKOUT NUTRITION
-----------------------
The anabolic window is wider than once thought — aim to eat within 2 hours of training. Target a 3:1 or 4:1 carb-to-protein ratio for endurance athletes; a 2:1 ratio for strength athletes. Example: chocolate milk, rice cakes with whey protein shake, or chicken and sweet potato.

CALORIC TARGETS
----------------
Weight loss: Maintain a moderate calorie deficit of 300–500 kcal/day. Aggressive deficits above 750 kcal/day risk muscle loss.
Muscle gain (bulk): Eat at a calorie surplus of 200–400 kcal/day to minimise fat gain.
Maintenance: Match total daily energy expenditure (TDEE). Use Mifflin-St Jeor equation to estimate BMR; multiply by activity factor (sedentary 1.2, lightly active 1.375, moderately active 1.55, very active 1.725).

MEAL TIMING FOR MUSCLE GROWTH
-------------------------------
Distribute protein intake evenly across 4–5 meals per day, targeting 25–40 g of protein per meal. This maximises muscle protein synthesis (MPS) compared to front- or back-loading.
