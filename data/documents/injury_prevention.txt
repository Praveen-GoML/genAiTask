FITMIND AI — INJURY PREVENTION & MANAGEMENT
=============================================

WARM-UP PROTOCOLS
------------------
A proper warm-up elevates core temperature, increases joint lubrication, activates the nervous system, and mentally prepares the athlete. It reduces injury risk significantly and improves performance.

General warm-up (5–10 min): light cardio (rowing, jump rope, bike) to raise heart rate and body temperature.
Dynamic mobility flow (5–10 min): leg swings, hip circles, arm circles, inchworms, world's greatest stretch, thoracic rotations.
Movement prep (5–10 min): progressive warm-up sets specific to the day's main lift. Rule: 2–3 sets at 50–60% of working weight, never start heavy.

THE BIG THREE INJURY-PRONE MOVEMENTS
--------------------------------------
Lower Back (Deadlift/Squat):
  - Cause: Lumbar flexion under load (rounded lower back).
  - Prevention: Strengthen the glutes, hamstrings, and erector spinae. Never load a movement pattern your mobility doesn't support yet.
  - McGill Big 3 for spine stability: modified curl-up, side bridge, bird-dog.

Knee (Squat/Lunge):
  - Cause: Valgus collapse (knees caving inward), patellofemoral overload, sudden volume spikes.
  - Prevention: Hip abductor and glute strengthening (banded clamshells, lateral band walks). Never increase weekly volume by more than 10%.
  - Signs of concern: sharp pain under the kneecap, swelling after training, clicking with pain.

Shoulder (Pressing/Pulling):
  - Cause: Internal shoulder impingement, rotator cuff overuse, poor scapular stability.
  - Prevention: Balance pressing and pulling volume (1:1 ratio minimum; 1:1.5 is better). Include face pulls, band pull-aparts, and external rotation work 3x/week.
  - Warning signs: pain at the front of the shoulder on internal rotation, pain at night lying on the shoulder.

COMMON OVERUSE INJURIES
-------------------------
Shin Splints (Medial Tibial Stress Syndrome)
  Common in: runners, especially beginners or those returning after a break.
  Prevention: Gradual mileage progression (10% per week max), proper footwear, calf strengthening, run on softer surfaces initially.
  Management: Rest, ice, compression. Calf raises on the edge of a step for eccentric strengthening.

Tendinopathy (Patellar, Achilles, Rotator Cuff)
  Tendon injuries are not inflammatory in the traditional sense — they are degenerative. Heat, not ice, is better for chronic tendinopathy.
  Gold standard treatment: heavy slow resistance training (HSR) for the affected tendon — eccentric exercises (e.g., single-leg calf raise for Achilles) 3 sets x 15 reps, very slowly.
  Avoid complete rest — tendons heal best with controlled loading.

IT Band Syndrome
  Common in: cyclists and runners.
  Cause: Not a tight IT band (the band can't be stretched) — it is hip abductor weakness causing poor mechanics.
  Fix: Strengthen glutes (especially gluteus medius), improve hip drop pattern, reduce mileage temporarily.

RETURN TO TRAINING AFTER INJURY
---------------------------------
Pain scale rule: Train at or below 3/10 on pain scale. Above 3/10 — reduce load.
No pain does not mean healed — tendons and cartilage take 3–6 months to fully remodel even after pain resolves.
Linear return: Rest → Pain-free movement → Bodyweight → Light load → Progressive loading.
Consult a physiotherapist for any injury involving sharp pain, swelling, locking, or instability. FitMind AI does not replace professional medical assessment.

MOBILITY & FLEXIBILITY ESSENTIALS
-----------------------------------
Hip flexors (kneeling lunge stretch): 60 seconds each side — critical for squatters and desk workers.
Thoracic extension (foam roller): 30–60 seconds on mid-back — essential for overhead work.
Ankle dorsiflexion (knee-to-wall test): Limited ankle mobility is the root cause of many squat problems. Work on ankle mobility daily with banded ankle mobilisations.
Hamstring flexibility: 90/90 position or supine leg raise. Tight hamstrings increase lower back injury risk under load.
